Who is affected by the transfer from primary and lower secondary school to upper secondary, what they need, and where it chafes today. The insight comes from the mapping of the current process in Bergen kommune and Vestland county authority, Novari's requirements for the transfer, and Oslo kommune's insight work.

## The use case

The pilot builds on [case 2 – Transition from primary and lower secondary to upper secondary](/en/behov/use-cases/02-overgang-grunnskole-vgo/). The case is broader than the pilot: it also covers information about the pupil's schooling, adaptations, measures and follow-up. Phase 1 was limited to the transfer of the pupil list (Vigo 1) in November, which prepares the pupils for applying to upper secondary in January. Phase 2 will include grades (Vigo 2 and 3), while the broader pupil journey sets the direction.

## The users and what they need

### The pupil and parents

The pupil is applying for upper secondary education and needs the information about them to be correct and to arrive on time, without having to carry it between the schools themselves. Parents need to be confident that the child receives the right follow-up in the transition. Pupils and parents see little of the transfer itself, but notice it when something goes wrong, for example an incorrect points basis, or a pupil who drops out and is only discovered when applications open.

### The school administration in primary and lower secondary school

Administrative staff, the head teacher, the head of year 10 and the school counsellor currently download the pupil list from the school system, correct it locally and upload it to VIGO Skole. They need:

- a simple, automated transfer, without storing files temporarily on their own PC
- to know that the transfer is complete and accepted by the county authority
- understandable feedback on errors, and the ability to correct them in their own system and resend straight away
- easy access, without having to hunt for the person who has login rights to VIGO Skole

The task is simple to begin with: one report per school, about 10 minutes. Bergen kommune has 26 municipal schools with year 10.

### The admissions office at the county authority

In November the admissions office sends the schools a notice about reporting, with a deadline for submission, keeps track of whether the schools upload the files, and moves them to Vigo (about 5 minutes per school). The work takes several days. They need:

- complete and correct data sets from all schools
- an overview of which schools have delivered, so that errors can be corrected early
- control of information that arrives after the deadline, for example appeals

The county authorities currently find the process orderly, with good overviews.

## Pain points

**In the current process:**

- It is frustrating to find login rights to VIGO Skole, and who at the school has them. Staff turnover at the schools makes this worse. If nobody has access, the school has to contact the admissions office.
- Storing files temporarily between two systems feels unnecessary when it ought to happen automatically.
- Locally stored files are a risk of data going astray.
- Primary and lower secondary schools are not as close to Vigo as upper secondary schools.
- Deadlines are short, and the summer is especially critical, when final grades are submitted.
- A short distance to the schools that hold the data is important when something needs correcting.

**Variants that do not follow the standard:**

- A few public and private schools have no school administration system. They log in to VIGO Skole and register national identity numbers manually.
- Pupils with fictitious identity numbers, for example newly arrived in Norway, must be entered manually in Vigo. The schools delete them from the file and report them to the admissions office by encrypted email.
- When the school and VIGO have registered the same fictitious identity number, term and final assessment grades (Vigo 2 and 3) can be transferred directly.
- Pupils and changes reported after the first file are handled manually, with the school contacting the admissions office.
- For home-schooled pupils it is unclear who sends the data: the municipality, the school or the parents. This applies to few pupils.

**From Oslo kommune's insight work**, which is relevant to SAMT-BU in general: fear of sharing, analogue sharing and manual work, inconsistent training, unclear roles and responsibilities, weakened user involvement and trust, lack of standardisation, fragmented systems, high dependence on individuals, and uncertainty about what can actually be shared. See [Legal matters](/en/pilotering/pilot-1/juridiske-forhold/#what-the-pilot-learned).

The requirements Novari has set for a digital transfer are described under [Current situation](/en/pilotering/pilot-1/naasituasjon/#requirements-for-a-digital-transfer).

## The dream journey

The dream journey, the transition as it would be in a perfect world, was a work package in phase 1 but was not completed. The experience report notes that dream journeys take longer to create than planned. In phase 2 it is deliverable L2 (desired user journey), together with L1 (current user journey).
